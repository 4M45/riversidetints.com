Window Tint Darkness Guide

Understanding tint percentages is essential before choosing the right shade. The VLT (Visible Light Transmission) number tells you how much light passes through — the lower the number, the darker the tint. Here's a quick breakdown from our Riverside tint experts:

5%

Limo Tint

The darkest available — blocks 95% of light. Nearly impossible to see through from outside. Popular for rear windows and privacy-focused builds. 5% tint is darker than 10% and is the go-to choice for maximum privacy.

20%

Dark Tint

Our most popular shade — offers a sleek, dark appearance with excellent privacy and heat rejection. 20% is darker than 35% and ideal for customers who want style without going full limo.

35%

Medium Tint

A popular choice that provides good heat rejection and a clean look. While 35% tint is not legal on front side windows in California (which require 70% VLT), it's perfect for rear and back windows.

50%

Light Tint

Blocks 50% of light while still maintaining great visibility. Perfect for drivers who want heat and UV protection without significantly darkening the glass. Factory tint on many vehicles.

Frequently Asked Questions

Get answers to the most common window tinting questions from the experts at Riverside Tints. Whether you're wondering about pricing, tint darkness levels, or California tint laws, we've got you covered.

Is $100 for 2 window tint good?

Paying $100 for two windows of tint can be a reasonable deal for basic dyed film, but you should be cautious about quality. At that price point, many shops use lower-grade film that may bubble, fade, or turn purple within a year or two. At Riverside Tints, we believe in using quality ceramic or carbon films that block more heat and UV rays. Our two-window packages start around $80–$150 depending on the vehicle and film grade, so $100 can be a fair price if the shop uses a reputable brand with a warranty. Always ask what brand and type of film is being used before committing.

Is 35% or 20% tint darker?

20% tint is darker than 35% tint. The percentage refers to Visible Light Transmission (VLT) — the amount of light allowed through the film. A 20% tint blocks 80% of visible light, while a 35% tint blocks only 65%. At Riverside Tints, 20% is one of our most popular choices for customers who want a sleek, dark look with solid privacy. Meanwhile, 35% offers a medium shade that works great on rear windows and is a popular factory look.

What is darker, 25 or 30 tint?

25% tint is darker than 30% tint. Remember, the lower the VLT percentage, the darker the film. A 25% tint allows only 25% of visible light through, while a 30% tint allows 30%. The difference between the two is subtle to the naked eye, but noticeable when placed side-by-side. At Riverside Tints, we keep both shades in stock and can show you live samples on your vehicle so you can pick the perfect darkness level for your needs.

Which tint is darker, 5 or 10?

5% tint (often called "limo tint") is darker than 10% tint. A 5% VLT film blocks 95% of visible light, making it nearly impossible to see inside the vehicle from the outside. A 10% tint still appears very dark but allows slightly more visibility. Both are popular choices for rear windows and the back windshield. At Riverside Tints, we install both shades — just keep in mind that 5% tint is not legal on front side windows in California. Our team will advise you on which windows can safely use these darker tints.

Can cops tell if your tint is too dark?

Yes, law enforcement officers can usually spot illegal tint visually and may use a tint meter to measure VLT on the spot during a traffic stop. In California, front side windows must allow at least 70% VLT, while rear side windows and the back windshield can be any darkness. The windshield can have non-reflective tint on the top 4 inches. At Riverside Tints, we always inform customers about California tint laws (Vehicle Code Section 26708) before installation so you can make an informed decision and avoid costly fix-it tickets that range from $25 to $197.

Is 2 days enough for tint?

Two days is generally enough initial drying time in warm, sunny conditions like Riverside's climate, but full curing takes 2–4 weeks. During the curing period you may notice small water bubbles or a hazy appearance — this is completely normal and will clear up as moisture evaporates through the film. At Riverside Tints, we recommend: keep windows rolled up for at least 48 hours, avoid car washes for 5–7 days, don't use window cleaner with ammonia, and avoid touching the inside film surface. Parking in direct sunlight helps tint cure faster.

Is a 35% tint illegal?

In California, 35% tint is illegal on the front side windows (driver and passenger), which must allow at least 70% VLT per the California Vehicle Code. However, 35% tint is perfectly legal on the rear side windows and the rear windshield. The windshield can have non-reflective tint on the top 4 inches only. Many of our Riverside Tints customers choose 35% on the rear windows for a clean, factory-like appearance while staying fully compliant. Tint laws vary by state, so if you're relocating, check the regulations in your new state.

How much does 3M tint cost?

3M window tint typically costs between $200 and $800 for a full vehicle, depending on the specific product line, number of windows, and vehicle size. 3M Crystalline (their premium ceramic tint) ranges from $400–$800, while 3M FX-Premium and Color Stable run $200–$500. At Riverside Tints, we carry premium ceramic and carbon tint films that rival or exceed 3M quality at competitive prices, and all our installations include a lifetime warranty. Contact us for a free custom quote based on your vehicle.

What helps tint cure faster?

The best way to help window tint cure faster is to park your vehicle in direct sunlight. Heat and sun exposure accelerate the evaporation of moisture trapped between the film and glass. Here in Riverside, our warm climate helps tint cure faster than in cooler or humid regions. Other tips from our experts at Riverside Tints: keep windows rolled up for at least 48 hours, avoid touching the inside film surface, skip car washes for 5–7 days, and never use window cleaners containing ammonia. Full curing typically takes 2–4 weeks, after which the tint will look crystal clear.
